## § 1  Purpose

¹ This Act gives effect to Article 9 § 8, by which every person may require the erasure of their personal data.

² It also gives effect to Article 9 § 6 ³, by which every query against a person's data is recorded and visible to that person.

## § 2  What the Republic holds

¹ The register holds identifiers and public keys. It holds no name, no address, no age and no contact of any kind — Article 9 § 6 ².

² Anything a person has told the Republic about themselves is held apart, in a store that is not published and not committed to the repository.

³ The Republic collects nothing it does not need for a stated purpose, and states the purpose when it collects.

## § 3  Erasure

¹ A person MAY require erasure at any time, by a signed record or by any means that identifies them.

² Erasure removes from the separate store everything linking the person to their identifier.

³ Erasure is performed within seven days and is confirmed to the person.

## § 4  What erasure does not do

¹ Erasure removes no record from the register, and breaks no hash — Article 9 § 8 ².

² The identifier remains, and every act done under it remains, attributed to the identifier and to nobody.

³ This is not a limitation on the right. It is what the right means in a Republic whose records cannot be altered: the person is unlinked, not unmade.

## § 5  Erasure and citizenship

¹ Erasure does not end citizenship, and citizenship does not require the Republic to hold anything about the person.

² A citizen may hold a citizenship about whose holder the Republic knows nothing at all, and this is the ordinary case rather than the exception.

## § 6  Access

¹ A person MAY require a statement of everything held about them, and it is given within seven days.

² Every access to the separate store is recorded, with who accessed it and why, and the record is visible to the person concerned — Article 9 § 6 ³.

³ The Auditor SHALL report each year the number of accesses, by whom, and on what ground.
