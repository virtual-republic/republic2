## § 1  Purpose

¹ This Act gives effect to Article 4 § 3, by which every entity has a charter, an organ holds only the authority the charter confers, and no charter may be inconsistent with the Constitution.

## § 2  The charter

¹ Every entity MUST have a published charter. An entity without one may not act, and its organs hold nothing.

² A charter states the entity's name and type, its purpose, who may be a member, how it decides, its organs and what each may do, and how it is dissolved.

³ A charter takes effect on publication, and every version remains published.

## § 3  What a charter may do

¹ A charter MAY narrow what the entity's type allows.

² A charter MUST NOT widen it. A provision purporting to confer a capacity the type withholds is of no effect, and the tools of the Republic will not give it effect.

³ In particular, a charter cannot give an entity the power to issue instruments where its type withholds that power, and cannot exempt an entity from publication.

## § 4  How an entity decides

¹ A charter states whether the entity decides by its members, each having one vote, or by its holders in proportion to what they hold.

² An entity that decides by members MUST treat every member alike. Weighting by seniority, by contribution or by anything else is of no effect.

³ A charter states the quorum and the threshold, and the figures it states are those the tools apply.

⁴ A resolution of an entity is published with its ballots, and any person may reproduce the count.

## § 5  Organs

¹ An organ acts for the entity within the authority the charter confers and no further — Article 4 § 3 ².

² An act of an organ beyond that authority is of no effect, and the entity is not bound by it.

³ An organ is filled by a resolution of the entity, and a charter states for how long.

⁴ A person dealing with an entity is entitled to rely on the published charter, and on nothing said outside it.

## § 6  Listing

¹ An entity whose type may issue instruments is not listed unless its charter says it is.

² An unlisted entity's instruments exist, are recorded, and pass directly between accounts. They are not traded on the exchange.

³ Listing is opened or closed by amending the charter, which is a decision of the entity's members or holders and not of its organs.

## § 7  Inconsistency

¹ Any citizen MAY put to the Court the question whether a charter is inconsistent with the Constitution.

² The Court MAY declare a provision of a charter of no effect, and the declaration is published.

³ The rest of the charter stands.
