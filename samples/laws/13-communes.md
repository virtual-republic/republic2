## § 1  Purpose

¹ This Act provides for communes, which under Article 4 § 1 ² exist only on a carried measure and are entered by the Registrar.

² It states what a commune is for, what a measure establishing one must say, and how one ends.

## § 2  What a commune is

¹ A commune is a standing body of citizens who have undertaken to do together something the Republic has not undertaken to do for them.

² It is not an organ of the Republic and exercises no power of the Republic.

³ It holds an account, may contract, and appears before the Court as any entity may.

⁴ A commune MUST NOT issue instruments, and no charter may give it that power — Article 4 § 2 ³.

## § 3  Establishing one

¹ A measure establishing a commune MUST state its name, its purpose, and the citizens founding it.

² The measure is of the class of ordinary measure.

³ On its carrying, the Registrar enters the commune, citing the measure — Article 4 § 1 ².

⁴ The Registrar SHALL enter it within seven days, and MUST NOT refuse a commune the Assembly has established.

## § 4  Membership

¹ Membership of a commune is open to any citizen, and is not refused on any ground listed in Article 3 § 2 ⁵.

² Every member of a commune is a coequal member. A commune MUST NOT weight its votes by holding, by seniority, or by anything else.

³ This section may not be narrowed by a charter. A body that weights its members differently is an association or a company, and should be formed as one.

## § 5  Its charter

¹ A commune's charter is subordinate to the measure establishing it, and may not enlarge its purpose.

² Where the measure is amended, the charter is read as amended to conform.

## § 6  Ending

¹ A commune is dissolved by resolution of two thirds of its members, by repeal of the measure establishing it, or by judgment of the Court.

² On dissolution its holdings pass to the Treasury — Article 4 § 4 ² — and no member takes any part of them.

³ A commune that has had no member for ninety days is dissolved, and the Registrar records it.
